Why You Got the Letter

You Didn't Do Anything Wrong. The Rules Changed.

After years of major storm losses, Texas insurers have quietly tightened how they treat older roofs. Here's what's actually behind that notice.

1

Carriers are re-rating roof age

Many insurers now decline to renew, or move to reduced "actual cash value" coverage, once a roof passes a certain age — often around 15 to 20 years. It's not about your specific roof; it's a policy-wide shift, and the letter is the result.

2

The deadline is the real pressure

Non-renewal notices come with a date. Miss it, and you're shopping for coverage with an older roof already on record — which usually means fewer options and higher premiums. The date is what turns a letter into an emergency.

3

You have more paths than "replace now"

Depending on your roof's actual condition, you may have real options — a documented certification, a targeted repair, a storm-damage claim if there's qualifying damage, or a planned replacement. But you can't choose a path until you know where the roof truly stands.

The Letter Has a Date on It. That Date Is the Whole Game.

Homeowners who act while the notice window is open keep their options open — certify, repair, claim, or plan a replacement on their own terms. The ones who wait past the deadline lose the easy paths and end up shopping for a new policy with an old roof on record. Same roof. Completely different outcome — decided by timing.
