Read This Before You Decide You're Fine

Why a Roof Can Look Perfect and Still Be Failing

Most homeowners walk outside after a storm, see no missing shingles, and assume their roof is fine. Here's what's actually happening up there.

1

Hail bruises — it doesn't break

A hailstone slams the shingle hard enough to crack the mat and knock off the protective granules, but it rarely leaves a hole. From the ground, the shingle looks completely normal. Underneath, the waterproof seal is broken.

2

Wind lifts — it doesn't tear

High wind doesn't always rip shingles off. More often it lifts the edges just enough to break the adhesive seal, then sets them back down. They look fine — but they're no longer watertight.

3

The damage stays silent for months

A broken seal doesn't leak immediately. It waits for the right combination of wind-driven rain. That's why damage from a spring storm often doesn't show up inside the house until fall — long after you'd connect it to the storm.
