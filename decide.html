The problem

So most people guess. And each guess has a price.

They file. Or they pay. Or they wait. All three go wrong the same way: without the real number.

They fileBecause a guy at the door said to. Then the check barely clears the deductible, or the roof gets depreciated for its age, and now there's a claim on the house for a roof they mostly paid for anyway.
They payAnd call three roofers, and get three numbers that are $12,000 apart, with no way to tell which one is honest. So the folder goes in a drawer.
They waitAnd water that gets past old shingles sits in the wood. Replace a roof before that and you're buying a roof. After, you're buying a roof plus carpentry. And more carriers every year want it replaced before they'll renew.

The math

The decision is one subtraction. You only have half of it.

What your roof actually costs to replace?
Minus your wind-and-hail deductible1% to 2% of home value on many Texas policies. Often $4,000 to $8,000.you know this
What filing would actually save you?

If the top number is well above your deductible and there's real storm damage, filing may make sense. If it's close, you're better off knowing what a monthly payment looks like. If the roof has years left, you should hear that too. None of it is knowable from a range online.

Why a search can't tell you

Three things set that top number. None of them show up online.

  1. 1
    How big the roof actually is. Not the house.Pitch and overhangs mean two houses that look identical from the street can differ by 800 square feet of shingle. Your home's square footage tells a roofer almost nothing.
  2. 2
    How steep it is, and how many layers are already up there.A second layer has to come off before anything new goes on. That's a day of labor and a dumpster before the first new shingle is nailed down.
  3. 3
    What the wood underneath looks like.Soft decking gets replaced board by board. Nobody can see it from the ground, from a satellite photo, or over the phone. Which is why a phone quote so often doubles once someone is actually on the roof.
Your number doesn't exist until somebody is standing on your roof. And your decision doesn't exist until you have your number.

What he will not do

He won't "waive" your deductible, "cover" it, or "work it into the estimate." Texas law says the deductible is yours to pay, and any roofer offering otherwise is offering something illegal, usually right before knocking on your neighbor's door.

He also won't file for you or promise what your insurance company will decide. That's theirs to decide. His job is to give you the real number so you can decide.
